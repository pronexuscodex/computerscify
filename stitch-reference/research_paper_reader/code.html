Abstract

Recent advancements in Large Language Models (LLMs) have revolutionized the field of natural language processing. This paper provides a comprehensive review of the current landscape, focusing on architectural innovations, training methodologies, and emergent properties. We propose a novel taxonomy that categorizes LLMs based on their objective functions and scale.

1. Introduction

The evolution from simple n-gram models to sophisticated transformer-based architectures marks a paradigm shift in machine learning. As scale increases, LLMs exhibit capabilities such as few-shot learning and complex reasoning that were previously unforeseen in smaller models.

This survey aims to bridge the gap between architectural theory and practical implementation by dissecting the underlying mechanisms of self-attention and parameter-efficient fine-tuning.

2. Methodology

Our data collection involved screening over 5,000 papers published between 2018 and 2024. We utilized a multi-stage filtering process to identify key influential works that significantly moved the state-of-the-art.

3. Scaling Laws

Kaplan et al. (2020) demonstrated that performance scales predictably with compute, dataset size, and parameter count. However, the "Chinchilla" scaling laws later suggested that many models were undertrained relative to their size. This realization shifted the focus from purely increasing parameter counts to optimizing the data-to-parameter ratio.

4. Emergent Properties

One of the most fascinating aspects of LLMs is the emergence of "zero-shot" capabilities. Without explicit training on a task, large models can perform translation, summarization, and even coding tasks by simply predicting the next token.

  • In-context learning via prompting.
  • Chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning.
